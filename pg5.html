Cookies the European Way

Laws and Bureaucracy(Justice Lens)

  • Opt in only cookies fixes part of the issue of users agreeing to terms they don’t understand, at least in theory. In a perfect world, everyone reads every line of a EULA
  • Companies are legally protected in their endeavors by simply asking. They are still allowed to run ads as well with no particular restrictions
  • Developers are allowed to have fully functioning websites as is current with no major changes
  • Users are “informed” of the use of cookies on any given website
  • Developers can still make usable websites, but may not be able to make as much advertising money through them

This path challenges the current status quo the least, the only change being a yes/no question at the start of the website. This has the least negative impact, even a potentially “pure positive” feedback, but as I mentioned above, that is far too idyllic for the real world. The current systems we have for “informed consent” online are crude and intentionally dense, still leaving users disadvantaged.
